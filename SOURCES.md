# Research sources

This register covers scientific and mission facts found in the reviewed baseline game. It is a source list, not a claim that every sentence in the game is a direct quotation. The final HTML should be checked against this list and any newly introduced fact should receive an appropriate source before submission.

## Mars facts and environment

- [Mars at a Glance — NASA/JPL](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/press_kits/insight/launch/facts/mars-at-a-glance/) — Martian day length, orbital period, atmospheric composition and pressure, temperature range, moons, Olympus Mons, and other basic planetary facts.
- [Mars Fact Sheet — NASA/NSSDC](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/marsfact.html) — planetary size, gravity, number of moons, and average solar irradiance at Mars compared with Earth.
- [Mars 2020 Quick Facts — NASA/JPL](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/press_kits/mars_2020/landing/quick_facts/) — Mars size, gravity, year, day length, atmospheric density, and temperature.
- [Mars Communications Disruption and Delay — NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/mars-communications-disruption-and-delay.pdf) — communication delay and communication blackout considerations for Mars missions.
- [CHAPEA 2: Audio Log 3 — NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/podcasts/houston-we-have-a-podcast/chapea-2-audio-log-3/) — explains that one-way Earth–Mars radio signal delay varies with planetary positions; the episode describes a 13–22 minute range in its example.

## Curiosity, Gale Crater, and surface resources

- [Curiosity Science Highlights — NASA Science](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/msl-curiosity/science-highlights/) — evidence for ancient rivers and lakes in Gale Crater, the Yellowknife Bay environment, clay minerals, organic carbon, and radiation concerns for human exploration.
- [What Is the Chemistry and Mineralogy Instrument? — NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/missions/mars-science-laboratory/curiosity-rover/what-is-the-chemistry-and-mineralogy-instrument/) — Curiosity's analyses of Rocknest, Yellowknife Bay, Glen Torridon, and Vera Rubin Ridge.
- [Five Bites Into Mars — NASA Science](https://science.nasa.gov/resource/five-bites-into-mars/) — Curiosity's 2012 collection of sand samples at Rocknest.
- [NASA Mars Rover Fully Analyzes First Soil Samples — NASA/JPL](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/nasa-mars-rover-fully-analyzes-first-soil-samples/) — the mineralogical composition of Rocknest samples; useful context for accurately describing Martian soil.
- [In-Situ Resource Utilization — NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/jsc-in-situ-resource-utilization/) — technologies for extracting and processing resources from lunar and Martian environments.

## Mission examples used in the game

- [Phoenix — NASA/JPL](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/missions/phoenix/) — Phoenix's confirmation of subsurface water ice on Mars.
- [NASA's Oxygen-Generating Experiment MOXIE Completes Mars Mission — NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/solar-system/nasas-oxygen-generating-experiment-moxie-completes-mars-mission/) — MOXIE's total oxygen production, 16th run, and oxygen purity results.
- [Veggie Fact Sheet — NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/veggie_fact_sheet_508.pdf) — lettuce grown and harvested aboard the International Space Station.
- [Opportunity — NASA Science](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/mer-opportunity/) — Opportunity's 2018 loss of communication during a planet-wide dust storm.
- [A Dust Devil on Hilly Terrain — NASA Science](https://science.nasa.gov/photojournal/a-dust-devil-on-hilly-terrain/) — dust devils and wind events can remove dust from rover solar panels.

## Source maintenance

When the game changes, check every fact shown in dialogue, location descriptions, collection cards, tooltips, and end-of-mission reports. Add the primary source used for each new or revised factual claim. Cite sources even when they are public, open access, or openly licensed, as required by the NASA Space Apps participant terms.
